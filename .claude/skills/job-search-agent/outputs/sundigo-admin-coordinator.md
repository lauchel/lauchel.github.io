## The decisive issue: specialty occupation

H-1B requires the ROLE to normally require a bachelor's degree in a SPECIFIC specialty.
Administrative Coordinator positions are among the most commonly denied categories,
because the duties listed (scheduling, records, correspondence, tracking requests)
do not require a particular degree. The posting's own Required list asks for language
fluency, organization, and Microsoft Office. No degree field is specified.

Consequence: even a willing employer likely could not successfully petition for this
title. Taking the job for visa reasons would probably not solve the visa problem.

## Compounding risk factors

- Company founded 2026. New employers face heightened USCIS scrutiny on ability to pay

- Aggregator metadata is unreliable here: the listing is also tagged "Mechanical
  Engineering," which matches nothing in the posting.
- Employer-provided housing plus employer-controlled immigration status creates high
  switching costs. Not inherently improper, but worth entering with open eyes.
- Lyerly, GA (pop. ~500, rural Chattooga County) has effectively no local finance market.
  Limited fallback if the role or the company does not work out.
- Career track: an administrative title would interrupt an FP&A analyst progression that
  currently has real proof points. Hard to explain, hard to reverse early career.

## Verdict

Do not apply. Confirm the specialty occupation point with an immigration attorney
before considering any similar administrative-titled posting marketed as H-1B friendly.
